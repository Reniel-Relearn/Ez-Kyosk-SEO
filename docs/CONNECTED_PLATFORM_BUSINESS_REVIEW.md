## Why not force a four-step pipeline?

The owner's ordering -> payments -> queue dashboard -> analytics direction identifies clear business capabilities, but omits the already owner-confirmed inventory scope and leaves POS ambiguous. Retaining five resolves those gaps without a generic extensions bucket. If four cards are later preferred, inventory can move to another section, but that is a real visibility tradeoff, not automatic simplification.

This section is a connected capability overview, not proof of a rigid runtime sequence. A useful conceptual explanation is: capture an order -> handle checkout/payment -> coordinate queued orders and their progress. Exact payment/queue timing depends on the deployment; do not claim all orders require staff checkout, all queues start only after payment, or automatic synchronization without confirmation. Inventory supports the ordering/operations flow; reporting draws on configured data across it. They are not two final steps that every individual customer performs.
